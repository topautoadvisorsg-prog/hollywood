# Torrison Drive — Client Vision Brief

**Source:** Customer discovery transcript supplied 28 July 2026  
**Purpose:** Convert the customer’s actual language into binding brand, UX, content, and commercial direction  
**Evidence rule:** “Explicit” means stated by the customer. “Implication” means a design or business conclusion drawn from those statements.

---

## 1. The Customer’s Actual Goal

### Explicit

The property currently performs consistently as an Airbnb and approximately supports its carrying costs, but the customer wants it to produce more than “hold its own.” She sees higher-margin opportunities in:

- Respectful private events
- Corporate events
- Photo shoots
- Music videos
- Film and other production uses
- Potential weddings when the property and pool configuration support them

She wants to retain the property long term because she considers it unusually special and difficult to replace.

### Implication

The website is not primarily an Airbnb marketing site. It is a **property brand and demand-generation platform** whose commercial priority is:

1. Shoot
2. Gather
3. Stay

Stay remains important because it supplies recurring occupancy and credibility, but the website should create higher-value direct opportunities around the location.

---

## 2. The Defining Product Is the Impression

### Explicit

The customer repeatedly emphasized that the property’s value is not simply the house:

- “It’s the impression.”
- Visitors are “completely awestruck.”
- The effect begins when a visitor pulls up and exits the car.
- The location, elevation, view, and relationship to the city are the defining qualities.

### Implication

The website’s opening story should follow the arrival experience:

1. Approach and elevation
2. First reveal of the view
3. Relationship to Universal Studios and Studio City
4. Pool, terraces, and usable event/production areas
5. The house and guest spaces

The first screen should not lead with bedroom counts or generic “luxury living” language. It should communicate the physical reveal and emotional impact.

## 3. Positioning Language

### Explicit

The customer described the desired feeling as:

- “City away from the city”
- Relaxing
- Close to everything
- Between the Valley and West Hollywood
- High-end and luxurious
- “No dark theme”

### Binding positioning territory

**Primary idea:** A city away from the city.

Supporting language:

- Above the studios
- Between the Valley and West Hollywood
- At the center of Los Angeles, removed from its noise
- A private hillside setting for stays, shoots, and respectful gatherings
- The view begins the experience

### Visual consequence

- The overall site remains bright.
- Cinematic does not mean dark.
- Dusk and night photography may appear as individual scenes, but black or near-black cannot dominate the interface.
- Luxury should come from typography, spacing, photography, restraint, and completeness—not a dark palette.

## 4. Commercial Audience Priorities

## Shoot — Priority 1

The customer explicitly mentioned:

- Photo shoots
- Music videos
- Filming
- Existing music-video production at the property
- Influencers and recognizable visitors
- A desire for the property to become known within the industry

The Shoot experience must demonstrate:

- Visual range
- Views and skyline
- Pool and terraces
- Interiors and ADU
- Arrival and privacy
- Natural light and time-of-day changes
- Practical production suitability
- Existing production credibility when materials are approved

Primary action: **Request a location scout**

## Gather — Priority 2

The customer explicitly wants:

- Respectful events
- Corporate events
- Potential weddings
- Approximately 60–70 people as a reasonable event threshold
- No “crazy parties”

The Gather experience must:

- Say what types of events fit the property
- Set tone and behavioral expectations
- Present approximately 60–70 as a provisional, client-stated reasonable threshold pending operational verification
- Treat a pool cover as a future/conditional configuration, not a current amenity
- Show planning, access, sound, parking, vendors, and neighborhood respect

Primary action: **Request an event proposal**

## Stay — Priority 3

The customer said Airbnb demand is consistent but often last minute. A management company currently handles calendaring, bookings, and communication.

The Stay experience must:

- Preserve the Airbnb booking path
- Sell the property’s impression and location
- Support advance planning where possible
- Avoid promising direct booking or concierge capabilities that are not operationally confirmed

Primary action: **View availability**

## 6. Brand Requirement

### Explicit

The property does not currently have a name. The customer agreed that it should become a recognizable brand so industry contacts can refer to the location by name. She did not propose “White Oak” or another name.

### Implication

- **White Oak is a working agency concept, not an approved customer name.**
- Concept review should show the naming system but visibly label the name as provisional.
- Naming should support word-of-mouth among producers, artists, planners, influencers, and guests.
- The final name needs to be easy to say, search, remember, tag, and place on physical signage.

### Naming review criteria

1. Distinctive within Los Angeles hospitality and location markets
2. Easy spelling and pronunciation
3. Available domain/social handles
4. Connected to a real property feature, feeling, view, or location
5. Suitable for Stay, Shoot, and Gather
6. Not dependent on an inaccurate neighborhood claim

## 8. Concept Corrections

## Concept 1 — Editorial Residence

Keep:

- Supplied bright editorial mockup
- Asymmetric collages
- Luxury typography
- Strong view-first story

Change:

- Prioritize the arrival impression over the house itself.
- Replace unsupported oak-centered claims unless the physical tree is verified as a customer-approved brand anchor.
- Weight Shoot and Gather more strongly than Stay.

## Concept 2 — Luminous Cinema

Replace the former dark “Cinematic Escape” direction.

- Bright daylight and golden-hour foundation
- Warm white interface
- Dusk/night scenes used only as dramatic chapters
- “City away from the city” narrative
- Arrival → view → creative possibility → evening
- No black-dominant or dark-theme interface

## Concept 3 — Location Dossier

Keep:

- Maimon-style section completeness
- Peerspace-style activity routing
- Production/event facts
- Case studies, FAQs, and process

Change:

- Shoot appears first.
- Gather appears second.
- Stay appears third.
- Respectful-use and neighborhood-fit language becomes part of qualification.

## 9. Homepage Story Order

Recommended shared story:

1. **The impression** — view-first hero and brand proposition
2. **City away from the city** — location and access
3. **Choose your purpose** — Shoot, Gather, Stay
4. **The visual range** — pool, view, interior, terrace, ADU, night
5. **Shoot here** — creative applications and location-scout CTA
6. **Gather here** — respectful occasions and provisional scale
7. **Stay here** — five-bedroom combined property and availability
8. **Proof** — approved productions, events, guests, or factual credibility
9. **Complete gallery** — real property imagery
10. **Make the introduction** — purpose-specific inquiry

This order encodes the owner’s commercial objective rather than defaulting to hospitality.

---

## 10. Binding Guardrails

1. No dark-theme concept.
2. Shoot and Gather receive stronger homepage emphasis than Stay.
3. “City away from the city” is the primary emotional territory.
4. The arrival impression and view lead the story.
5. White Oak remains provisional until naming approval.
6. Event positioning emphasizes respectful, corporate, creative, and planned uses—not parties.
7. Capacity and property facts remain provisional until verified.
8. Existing production/influencer proof is used only with approval.
9. Pool-cover configurations are presented as future possibilities, not current capability.
10. The website’s success is measured by higher-value qualified inquiries, not only Airbnb clicks.
