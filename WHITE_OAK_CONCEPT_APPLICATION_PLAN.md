# White Oak — Three-Concept Application Plan

**Purpose:** Turn the competitor research into three complete, visibly different website concepts for customer review.  
**Property:** 7845 Torrison Drive  
**Audiences:** Stay · Shoot · Gather  
**Status:** Planning document; implementation follows approval of this comparison structure.

---

## 1. Outcome

The customer will receive three complete website directions. Each direction will have the same six real pages and the same property facts, but will demonstrate a different combination of visual hierarchy, storytelling, interaction, information density, and conversion behavior.

The review should allow the customer to say:

- “I prefer Concept 1’s homepage.”
- “I want Concept 2’s gallery.”
- “I like Concept 3’s event inquiry and facts.”
- “Keep Concept 1’s typography but use Concept 2’s full-width scenes.”

The goal is not to force one all-or-nothing selection. The review system should reveal preferences clearly enough to create a final hybrid without producing an incoherent mixture.

## 2. The Three Concepts

## Concept 1 — Editorial Residence

**Client-facing name:** White Oak House  
**Core idea:** A private architectural residence presented like an editorial feature.  
**Primary references:** Supplied Layout 1 mockup, Aman restraint, Boutique Homes editorial authority, Maimon Real Estate Agents page depth.  
**Best for:** Prestige, architecture, high-end stays, and emotional brand value.

### Defining features

- Bright porcelain background
- Oversized black editorial serif typography
- Fine champagne rules and deep forest accents
- Split compositions and asymmetric image collages
- Captions and typographic pauses
- Real pages with substantial image-and-copy sequences
- Benefits introduced after emotional value
- Case-study/proof section before the closing CTA
- Slow, restrained reveal motion

### What it demonstrates to the customer

- A light luxury palette
- Magazine-style storytelling
- Collage and overlapping photography
- Elegant rather than aggressive conversion
- A residence-first brand identity
- Maimon-style content completeness inside an editorial system

---

## Concept 2 — Cinematic Escape

**Client-facing name:** Above the City  
**Core idea:** One day and one night at the estate, told as a film.  
**Primary references:** Rosewood media sequencing, River Run event emotion, Maimon’s longer reveals, Aman’s sparse copy.  
**Best for:** Atmosphere, hospitality, private events, music/entertainment clients, and memorable presentation.

### Defining features

- Full-bleed scenes
- Photography provides most of the color
- Near-black and cloud-white transition sections
- Time-of-day chapter markers
- Optional muted video architecture with real-image poster fallback
- Controlled image crossfades
- Minimal copy over imagery
- Manual carousels and visible media controls
- Large scene changes followed by quiet information sections

### What it demonstrates to the customer

- Cinematic full-screen photography
- Dark/light pacing
- Emotional storytelling
- Time-based narrative
- Video-ready hero architecture
- Immersive event and gallery presentation

---

## Concept 3 — Location Dossier

**Client-facing name:** White Oak / Studio City  
**Core idea:** A beautiful private estate presented with the clarity of a premium production and event dossier.  
**Primary references:** Maimon Real Estate Agents page structure, Peerspace activity routing, Plum Guide trust architecture, Rosewood purpose segmentation.  
**Best for:** Shoots, events, planners, representatives, and high-intent customers.

### Defining features

- Strong modular grid
- Stay / Shoot / Gather routing immediately visible
- Annotated images and numbered sections
- Benefit modules and service cards
- Capacity, access, lighting, parking, and process information
- Case-study structure
- Searchable/filterable FAQ
- Downloadable location-packet placeholder
- Explicit inquiry and response expectations

### What it demonstrates to the customer

- Information-rich luxury
- Fast qualification
- Production and event usefulness
- Strong conversion structure
- Maimon-style long-form persuasion
- Peerspace-style audience/activity clarity without marketplace styling

## 4. Review-System UX

The comparison system is presentation software wrapped around three real sites. It is not part of the final customer-facing website.

### Persistent review bar

The top review bar contains:

- Concept 1 — Editorial Residence
- Concept 2 — Cinematic Escape
- Concept 3 — Location Dossier
- Current page name
- “Compare this page” action
- Optional “Save preference” action stored on the current device

Switching concepts preserves the current page:

- Concept 1 / Gallery → Concept 2 / Gallery
- Concept 2 / Host → Concept 3 / Host
- Concept 3 / Stay → Concept 1 / Stay

### Page comparison drawer

The drawer does not show three tiny unusable websites side by side. Instead it shows:

- One large active concept
- Two thumbnail previews
- A short “What changes here?” list
- Previous/next concept controls
- Preference prompts:
  - Layout
  - Typography
  - Photography treatment
  - Motion
  - Information density
  - CTA style

### Review prompts

At the end of each page:

1. Which composition feels most appropriate?
2. Is this too sparse, balanced, or too dense?
3. Which image treatment feels most premium?
4. Which CTA feels natural?
5. Which feature should carry into the final site?

No customer response is required to browse. Review notes are optional and device-local unless a real feedback backend is later requested.

### Final-site separation

The review bar, concept labels, comparison drawer, and preference controls are removed after direction approval. The final website keeps only the selected property navigation.

## 13. Image-Library Application Rules

The current 81 property photographs are enough for concept presentation.

### During visual review

- Exact room labels can remain provisional.
- A strong interior may temporarily illustrate living, hospitality, service, or production flexibility.
- A terrace image may illustrate Gather even without event attendees.
- A bedroom image may illustrate privacy or rest.
- No generated image should pretend to be the real property.
- No fake event crowd should be composited into the property without a visible “concept visualization” label.

### Before final production

- Verify room and amenity labels.
- Remove duplicate crops.
- Confirm event/production usage rights.
- Create desktop and mobile crop coordinates.
- Write truthful captions and alt text.
- Replace provisional case studies and testimonials with approved material.

## 15. Implementation Order

### Phase 1 — Foundation

1. Preserve all seven page routes.
2. Build shared content data for facts, images, FAQs, benefits, and CTAs.
3. Build the review bar and concept-preserving route switch.
4. Build shared accessible primitives: header, footer, media control, gallery dialog, accordion, form fields.

### Phase 2 — Representative pages

Build these first:

1. All three Home pages.
2. All three Shoot pages.
3. All three Gallery pages.

This set exposes the maximum contrast:

- Brand storytelling
- Emotional immersion
- Utility/information density
- Photography treatment
- Interaction style
- Conversion approach

Review these before completing the remaining pages.

### Phase 3 — Complete site directions

Build Estate, Stay, Gather, and Contact in all three concepts using the approved contrast level.

### Phase 4 — Review instrumentation

- Add preference prompts.
- Add device-local notes/favorites if desired.
- Add “What changes?” explanations.
- Verify switching preserves page and scroll starts at a predictable position.

### Phase 5 — QA and presentation

- Desktop: 1440 px
- Laptop: 1280 px
- Tablet: 768 px
- Mobile: 390 px
- Keyboard and reduced motion
- Image loading and broken-image fallback
- All route and CTA behavior
- Clear separation between prototype actions and live actions

## 16. Customer Presentation Sequence

Do not begin by letting the customer wander without context.

### Round 1 — Homepages

Present the same property story three ways:

1. Editorial
2. Cinematic
3. Dossier

Ask for reactions to composition, density, color, type, motion, and CTA tone.

### Round 2 — Shoot

Demonstrate the largest UX difference:

- Editorial persuasion
- Cinematic possibility
- Production qualification

Ask whether the customer values emotional presentation, rapid facts, or a blend.

### Round 3 — Gallery

Compare:

- Curated magazine
- Immersive sequence
- Filterable contact sheet

### Round 4 — Complete journeys

Walk one Stay journey and one Gather journey from homepage to inquiry in each surviving direction.

### Decision output

Record choices as a design recipe:

- Base concept
- Homepage composition
- Navigation
- Typography
- Palette
- Motion level
- Gallery model
- Shoot information density
- Gather case-study style
- Contact/inquiry model

This recipe becomes the final production specification.

---

## 17. Acceptance Criteria for the Concept Review

1. Seven real pages exist in every concept.
2. Every page contains 8–10 substantive sections.
3. The same page can be compared across all three concepts.
4. Concepts differ structurally, not merely cosmetically.
5. Existing property images are used throughout.
6. Each concept demonstrates exclusive signature features.
7. Stay, Shoot, and Gather have distinct content and forms.
8. No fake press, testimonial, client, or capacity claim is presented as fact.
9. Review controls are visibly separate from the property website.
10. Mobile designs are recomposed rather than simply stacked.
11. Keyboard, focus, media controls, and reduced motion are included.
12. The presentation produces a clear final design recipe.
