# White Oak Customer and Sales Presentation Guide

## Presentation objective

Use White Oak to show how a premium property can be interpreted in multiple ways, how customer preferences are captured, and how those choices become one final website.

Do not present the concepts as three templates for sale.

## Part 1 — Establish the challenge

Explain:

- White Oak serves stays, respectful events and productions.
- A conventional listing page could not express all three audiences well.
- The project therefore explored three distinct storytelling and UX directions.

## Part 2 — Show the three concepts

### Editorial Residence

Ask the viewer to notice:

- Typography
- Negative space
- Asymmetric composition
- Architectural authority
- Quiet invitation language

### Luminous Cinema

Ask the viewer to notice:

- Four-act narrative
- Full-screen photography
- Camera drift and opposing movement
- Soft dissolves
- Signature moments and pauses
- Optional ambience

### Location Dossier

Ask the viewer to notice:

- Audience routing
- Practical facts
- Visual range
- Qualification information
- Explicit actions

## Part 3 — Demonstrate feature choice

Compare the same page across concepts:

1. Home
2. Gallery
3. Estate or Host

Ask:

- Which hero establishes the strongest first impression?
- Which pacing feels appropriate?
- How much practical information should appear early?
- Which gallery interaction feels most premium?
- Which CTA feels natural rather than aggressive?
- Which motion should remain in the final site?

## Part 4 — Explain the design recipe

Show that the final result does not have to copy one concept completely.

Example:

- Editorial typography
- Luminous hero and chapter pacing
- Dossier property facts and inquiry structure
- Luminous gallery viewer
- Editorial closing invitation

Then explain how these selections are normalized into one coherent system.

## Part 5 — Show the final result

Once the customer-selected site is complete, present:

1. The three original directions.
2. The documented customer decisions.
3. The final design system.
4. The finished website.
5. The differences between concept prototypes and production behavior.

## Language for future prospects

Use:

> White Oak began with three intentionally different directions. The owner did not have to describe a design vocabulary from scratch; they could react to real layouts, pacing, galleries and interaction models. We documented those preferences and used them to direct one final experience.

Avoid:

- “Pick a template.”
- “We can copy this exact site for you.”
- “Every luxury property needs cinematic motion.”
- Claims that concept placeholders were part of the final customer website.

## Required labels

During review:

- Concept Presentation
- Prototype interaction
- Concept ambience
- Provisional fact
- Real footage drop-in ready

After launch:

- Clearly separate the archived concept presentation from the final customer website.
- Identify any confidential or unapproved customer information before using the case study publicly.

## Presentation completion record

After final approval, document:

- Date of concept presentation
- Stakeholders present
- Preferred base direction
- Accepted and rejected features
- Final decision recipe
- Launch URL
- Final screenshots
- Customer quote, only with approval
- Business or engagement outcomes, only when verified
